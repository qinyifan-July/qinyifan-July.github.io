TransComm: A New Passive IoT Communication Paradigm

2025-03-01
paper

June. 2023 – now

Contributions

  1. Introduced “TransComm,” a novel passive IoT communication paradigm that directly manipulates Tx-to-Rx signals instead of radiating energy like backscatter.
  2. Demonstrated through theoretical analysis and modeling that TransComm eliminates cascaded path loss, achieving 16.3 dB lower theoretical path loss compared to backscatter.
  3. Implemented ultra-low-power TransComm tags using low-power metamaterials, consuming only 5.3 uW per meta-atom when modulating 1 Mbps 802.11b signals.

Key Innovations:

  1. Direct Signal Manipulation: Avoids the cascaded path loss inherent in backscatter communication, significantly improving range and efficiency.
  2. Low-Power Metamaterials: Enables ultra-low-power operation, making TransComm suitable for energy-constrained IoT devices.

Experimental Results:

  1. Range Improvement: Achieved a full-coverage range of 80 meters, 8 times greater than traditional backscatter communication.
  2. Power Efficiency: Demonstrated energy consumption as low as 5.3 uW per meta-atom, ensuring long-term operation in passive IoT scenarios.

Applications:

  1. Long-Range IoT: TransComm’s extended range makes it ideal for applications requiring wide-area coverage.
  2. Energy-Efficient Devices: The ultra-low-power design is suitable for battery-less or energy-harvesting IoT devices.

Future Work:

  1. Optimization: Further refine the metamaterial design to reduce power consumption and improve signal integrity.
  2. Field Testing: Deploy TransComm in real-world IoT networks to evaluate its performance under diverse conditions.

OsTAG: A Novel Inductor_free LoRa Backscatter Design

2024-12-01
paper

May. 2024 – Dec. 2024

Contributions

  1. Research on improving the efficiency of LoRa backscatter communication by eliminating inductor-based impedance load arrays.
  2. Proposed “OsTAG,” a novel design that virtually creates oversampled impedance, reducing chip area by 72%.
  3. Developed prototypes and integrated circuits (ICs) to validate the design’s performance and efficiency.

Key Innovations:

  1. Virtual Oversampled Impedance: Eliminated the need for large-sized inductors in the impedance load array, reducing chip area significantly.
  2. Technical Challenges Overcome: Managed oversampling clock defects and mitigated approximation errors to maintain quantization performance.

Experimental Results:

  1. Area Reduction: OsTAG’s area is only 28% of existing chip designs.
  2. Cost Efficiency: Reduced production costs by optimizing the design for mass production.

Applications:

  1. IoT Communication: OsTAG’s ultra-low power consumption (tens of microwatts) makes it ideal for large-scale IoT applications.
  2. Integrated Circuits (ICs): The design’s compact size and efficiency are suitable for integration into modern ICs.

Future Work:

  1. Scalability: Further optimize the design for even smaller chip areas and lower power consumption.
  2. Real-world Deployment: Test OsTAG in various IoT scenarios to validate its robustness and reliability.

Design of Backscatter Tag Supporting OFDM Signal Generation

2023-05-01
paper

June. 2022 – May. 2023

Contributions

  1. Designed a backscatter communication label capable of generating OFDM signals by modulating the reflection coefficient through transistor bias voltage control.
  2. Proposed a novel approach where the label receives a pure carrier wave and reflects OFDM signals, eliminating the need for dual-channel usage and reducing requirements for the carrier transmitter.
  3. Highlighted the advantages of OFDM, including improved spectrum utilization and interference resistance in backscatter communication.

Key Innovations:

  1. OFDM Modulation in Backscatter: Enabled OFDM signal generation directly from a pure carrier wave, simplifying the system design.
  2. Single-Channel Operation: Avoided the need for dual-channel usage, reducing complexity and cost.

Experimental Results:

  1. Efficiency: Demonstrated the ability to generate OFDM signals with minimal power consumption.
  2. Flexibility: Showed compatibility with existing IoT communication frameworks.

Applications:

  1. Spectrum-Efficient IoT: Ideal for IoT applications requiring high spectrum efficiency and low interference.
  2. Low-Cost Devices: Suitable for cost-sensitive IoT deployments due to simplified hardware requirements.

Future Work:

  1. Optimization: Further enhance the label design for higher data rates and broader frequency ranges.
  2. Integration: Test the label in real-world IoT systems to evaluate its performance and scalability.
